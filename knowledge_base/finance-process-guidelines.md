# Finance Process Automation Guidelines

## Purpose
These guidelines describe how automation may support Finance Operations while preserving required controls and human accountability. Finance processes often contain repetitive work, but structured data does not automatically make a process suitable for full automation.

## Invoice Intake
Supplier invoices are received through approved channels and entered into the finance workflow. Automation may extract standard fields such as supplier name, invoice number, date, currency, purchase-order reference, and total amount.

Extracted information should be validated against available business records before it is used for downstream action. If required fields are missing, values conflict, or the supplier cannot be matched reliably, the invoice should be routed to a Finance employee.

## Exception Handling
Routine exceptions may be categorized automatically to support work routing. Examples include missing purchase-order reference, duplicate invoice suspicion, supplier master-data mismatch, amount variance, unsupported currency, and missing approval information.

Classification may help prioritize work, but automation should not independently approve a financial exception that requires employee judgment.

## Payment Approval
AI-generated recommendations must not directly approve or reject payments. Payment authorization remains subject to existing Finance approval rules and segregation-of-duties controls.

A workflow may prepare information for an approver, summarize an exception, or identify which approval path appears relevant. The final approval decision must remain with an authorized employee.

## Data Sensitivity
Supplier banking details, payment information, tax identifiers, and contractual financial data are considered sensitive. Prototype workflows using external AI services should use synthetic or sanitized invoice data unless approval for real financial data has been granted.

Operational logs should not copy full invoice content unless there is a clear business need.

## Reporting Automation
Recurring internal reporting is generally a strong automation candidate when calculation logic is already defined. Automation may collect structured data, apply approved transformations, format a recurring report, and distribute it internally.

If a report contains management commentary or interpretation, AI may draft text for review, but factual financial figures should come from deterministic source data rather than being generated by a language model.

## Controls and Monitoring
Finance automations should preserve existing approval boundaries rather than bypassing them. The process owner should confirm which steps are informational, which are deterministic, and which require human judgment.

Finance should periodically review automated exception classification, extraction errors, duplicate handling, and failed system integrations. Repeated errors affecting financial records should result in suspension of the affected automated path until the issue is understood.
